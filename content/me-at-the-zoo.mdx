Every so often I rewatch the first video ever uploaded to YouTube. It is nineteen seconds long. A man stands in front of some elephants and observes that they have really long trunks, and that that's cool, and then it ends.

<YouTube id="jNQXAC9IVRw" title="Me at the zoo — the first YouTube video" />

What gets me is not the historical trivia; it's the calibration. Two decades of production arms-race later — jump cuts, thumbnails engineered like slot machines — the founding document of the entire medium is a guy pointing a camera at a thing he likes. There's probably a lesson in there about this website.

The embed above is also the demo for the `<YouTube>` component: what you see before clicking is a thumbnail and a play button, nothing more. The actual YouTube iframe — and the megabyte of script it drags along — loads only when you press play.
